TACTICAL SYSTEMS · 03

Understanding the Darkness

The enemy system is the heart of solo play. The aim is to learn its triggers and priorities so you can predict risk without inventing enemy behaviour.

How to study enemy behaviour

1

Identify the trigger

What event causes the enemy procedure to activate? Check the scenario, phase sequence, cards and any special enemy rules.

2

Read the actual priority

Determine which enemy, target or action is selected and in what order. If priorities are listed, follow their order exactly.

3

Resolve legal behaviour

Apply movement, target eligibility, range, line of sight and special abilities. If a preferred action is illegal, use the rulebook's fallback rather than guessing.

4

Resolve all consequences

Apply attacks, damage, conditions, spawns and other triggered effects in the correct order, including any new triggers those effects create.

Build a threat map before acting

At the start of a decision, scan the board in this order:

  1. Immediate danger: enemies already able to attack or threaten your hero.
  2. Trigger risk: actions that activate the Darkness or advance a pressure track.
  3. Position: routes, chokepoints, terrain and objective access.
  4. Escalation: lairs, captains or other mechanisms that can make future turns harder.
  5. Recovery: legal opportunities to reposition, defend, refresh or restore resources.
Solo discipline: Resolve enemy actions as a procedure, not as a narrative choice. Predict the result before you act, then compare your prediction with the actual rules resolution. This is how the guide will develop reliable tactical examples.

Table-side enemy decision procedure

Use this as a provisional practice aid while learning the original rules. The sequence below is based on community reference material and must be checked against your original rulebook and the individual Darkness/Monster cards before being treated as definitive.

  1. Read the active Darkness card first. Record the activation order, any named enemy groups, spawn instructions and special exceptions. Do not substitute a remembered generic order for a card-specific instruction.
  2. Separate movement from attacks. The community reference describes resolving the activation step-by-step across the activated enemies, with movement completed before attacks. Verify the exact phase sequence in the rulebook you own.
  3. Choose a target from the enemy's printed priority. Work down the priority list in order. Only move to the next criterion when the previous one does not distinguish between eligible targets. Do not choose the most convenient target for the hero.
  4. Check whether the target is reachable. Trace legal movement through open squares, respecting movement points, occupied spaces, range and line of sight. If the preferred target cannot be reached, use the printed priority and rulebook fallback to select the next eligible target.
  5. Apply the enemy's movement style. The community guide distinguishes Instinct enemies, which can stop at the first suitable square and block followers, from Intellect enemies, which may manoeuvre to leave room for allies. Check the monster classification on the actual card.
  6. Resolve groups carefully. The community guide describes adjacent Minions of the same type moving as a group after a lead Minion (a “scout”) selects a target, while Captains, Agents, Mini-Bosses and Bosses choose and move individually. Confirm that this applies to the edition and card in play.
  7. Do not assume a ranged enemy stays still. The community reference says ranged enemies try to maintain attack range and line of sight, and may still attack another eligible target if they cannot attack their preferred target. Verify each enemy's own rules and card text.
  8. Resolve attacks and special effects only after positioning. Recheck the target, line of sight, attack range, attack values, conditions and any card-specific exceptions. Then resolve damage, deaths, and triggered effects in the specified order.
  9. Resolve spawns from the actual spawn instruction. Check the Lair, the spawn definition, available squares and the monster's priority. Do not place a spawn wherever it is easiest for the player.
Fair-play test: Before moving any enemy, write down or say (1) its priority target, (2) its legal route, (3) its final square, and (4) whether it can attack. Then execute that decision literally. If you cannot justify one of those steps from a rule or card, mark it “needs verification” rather than improvising.

Quick board scan for each enemy

  • Who activates now? Use the Darkness card and the rules' activation order.
  • What does it prioritise? Read that monster's own priority list, top to bottom.
  • Who is eligible? Check reachability, range, line of sight and any special targeting rules.
  • How does it move? Individual enemy or group; Instinct or Intellect; melee or ranged; printed movement value.
  • What happens next? Attack if legal, then apply damage, conditions, deaths, spawns and other triggered effects in rule order.
